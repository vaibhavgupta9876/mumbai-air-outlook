Why ventilation sets the level

PM2.5 in Mumbai is largely a mixing problem: the same emissions under a shallow, calm night build up to a higher concentration. We checked that against the data: for every station-day since February 2025, the night-time ventilation (boundary-layer height × wind, 20:00–07:00, from Open-Meteo) against that day's mean PM2.5 from the challenge data.

About

This page is an additional submission to the Google Mumbai Data Challenge (Zindi, 2026). It turns the challenge's forecasting task into something a resident can act on: which hours of which days to be outdoors, and how long the air takes to recover after Diwali.

How to read the outlook

The band is not a confidence interval from a formula. It is the pair of forecasts we actually submit, one scaled low and one scaled high around a five-model average (Chronos-2 zero-shot runs with weather covariates, a weather-driven gradient-boosting model, and covariate variants). In the backtests the daily level is the part a six-day forecast gets wrong most, so the band spans the level; the hourly shape inside a day is well predicted. The central line is the unscaled average.

Why ventilation

The ventilation index multiplies the forecast boundary-layer height by the 10 m wind speed. Its night-time terciles over 15 Oct to 15 Dec 2025 give the stagnant, mixed and ventilated labels. The "Why ventilation" tab shows how much it explains, and how much it does not.
